# Choosing a broker for a very small account (researched 2026-10-19)

What matters with 5 to 100 dollars: commission free trades, fractional shares (so a 10 dollar order can buy a slice of an expensive stock), no account minimum, no inactivity fees, a simple app, and good order types.

Brokers with commission free US stocks and ETFs and fractional shares: Fidelity (1 dollar minimum, no account minimum, well rated app), Charles Schwab (1 dollar minimum, strong for retirement accounts), Interactive Brokers Lite (1 cent minimum, built for advanced traders), J.P. Morgan Self-Directed (5 dollar minimum, handy for Chase customers), and Alpaca (commission free, fractional shares, built for software like JARVIS).

For Madam Swain's goals: Alpaca is the only one here that JARVIS can connect to directly, so he can watch the account, send alerts and line up orders for her to confirm. It is already connected as a practice account. Fidelity or Schwab are simpler everyday apps with strong support, but JARVIS cannot see them. Many people use one for learning with software and one for plain investing. There is no single best broker. Check each broker's current fees, funding steps and account approval on its own website, and fund only through the broker's own site.
