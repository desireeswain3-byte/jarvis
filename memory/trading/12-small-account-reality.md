# What small accounts can realistically do

Growth comes from three things: the money put in, the percentage return, and time. With tiny balances the dollar amounts are small even when the percentage is good. A strong day for a careful trader is often 0.5 to 2 percent, and many days are flat or negative.

Dollar examples at +1 percent on the day: 5 dollars gains 5 cents, 10 dollars gains 10 cents, 50 dollars gains 50 cents, 100 dollars gains 1 dollar, 1000 dollars gains 10 dollars. At +5 percent on a very good day: 5 dollars gains 25 cents, 50 dollars gains 2.50, 100 dollars gains 5 dollars.

Gaining 20 dollars on a 100 dollar account in a day is a 20 percent day. That takes big risk and usually ends in large losses. The realistic path to bigger dollar gains is to learn on the practice account, keep risk small, journal results, add money from income over time, and let the percentage compound. Never use borrowed money or leverage to chase bigger dollar numbers. Stocks can also lose value, and you can lose all of what you put in.
