# Swing trading

What it is: holding a stock for a few days to a few weeks to catch one move, using daily charts instead of minute charts. Day trading is her main plan; swing trading is the backup for slow days, busy weeks, or when the day-trade limit gets close.

Why it suits a small account: fewer trades, so the pattern day trader rule (4 or more day trades in 5 business days under $25k in a margin account) rarely matters, because a trade held overnight is not a day trade. Less screen time. In a cash account, the money from a sale settles the next business day, so plan trades around that.

The big risk is the overnight gap. A stock can open far below your stop after bad news, and the stop then sells at the open price, so the loss is bigger than planned. Keep swing positions small and never hold through an earnings report: check the earnings date before buying and sell before it if the trade is still open.

Only trade with the market. Swing longs only when SPY is above its 50 day average. When SPY is below it, sit out or keep size tiny.

Setup 1, pullback in an uptrend: the stock closes above its 50 day average and its 20 day average is rising. It pulls back to touch or come near the 20 day average, then has a day that closes up. Buy near that close. Stop just under the lowest low of the last few days.

Setup 2, breakout from a base: the stock has moved sideways for a few weeks, then closes above the highest high of the last 20 days on volume at least 1.5 times its normal daily volume. Buy near that close. Stop under the recent lows or under the middle of the base.

Risk: risk about 1 percent of the account per trade (the distance from entry to stop, times the shares). Skip a trade if the stop would have to be more than about 8 percent away; the setup is too loose. Hold one or two swing trades at a time at most.

Exits, decided before buying: take profit at 2 times the risk (2R). Once the trade is up 1R, move the stop to the entry price so it cannot turn into a loss. Use a time stop: if it has not worked in about 10 trading days, sell and free the money. Sell immediately if it closes below the stop level.

Broker detail: with fractional shares, Alpaca only accepts stop orders that last until that day's close. A swing stop must be put back in every morning. JARVIS's swing mode does this by itself.

Keep a journal of every swing trade: setup, entry, stop, target, result, and what the chart looked like. Judge the system on 20 or more trades, never on one.
