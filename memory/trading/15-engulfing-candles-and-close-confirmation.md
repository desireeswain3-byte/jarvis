# Engulfing candles and waiting for the candle to close

From videos Madam Swain shared (TikTok creators @bagborrowtrade, DE'JA on engulfing candles, @tomcampcoaching on the 4 hour chart, @seanmyers copying streamers, @tradingwith_tiara on small live accounts). Her takeaway from DE'JA: "it ain't no code, just started focusing on engulfing candles more."

What an engulfing candle is (standard definition): a bullish engulfing candle is a green candle whose body (open to close, not the wicks) completely covers the body of the red candle before it. It shows buyers overpowered sellers. A bearish engulfing candle is the opposite: a red body that completely covers the previous green body. On its own it means little; it matters when it forms at a key level (support for bullish, resistance for bearish) after a move into that level, and with above-normal volume.

Wait for the close (@bagborrowtrade): "Wait until the 15 min candle opens and closes above or below" the level. Do not trade a level just because price pokes through it. A wick through a level is not a break; only a full 15 minute candle closing beyond it counts. Her charts mark key levels first (the high and low of the earlier session, drawn as horizontal lines), wait for the 15 minute close beyond a level, then use the 5 minute chart to time the entry. Example in her video: Nasdaq micro futures broke below a marked level, and the trade only counted after a 15 minute candle closed below it.

Putting them together, a rule to test on the practice account: mark yesterday's high and low and the first 15 minutes' high and low. Long only when a 15 minute candle closes above a level AND that candle (or the next 5 minute candle) is a bullish engulfing candle on above-normal volume. Stop under the low of the engulfing candle. Target 2 times the risk. Skip it if the stop is wider than 3 percent.

Higher timeframe (@tomcampcoaching: "The 4 hour timeframe can change your life"): check the 4 hour chart for the direction first and only take day trades that go the same way. This matches the playbook rule that daily, 4 hour and 1 hour should agree.
